Layout considerations

What makes an ensuite different from a secondary bathroom

An ensuite gets used differently than a guest bathroom, and the layout should reflect that.

01

Two-person mornings need two sinks

A double vanity is the single biggest quality-of-life upgrade in most ensuite renovations, and it's worth the extra plumbing rough-in.

02

Tub and shower can usually coexist

If the footprint allows it, a separate soaker tub and walk-in shower give you both options instead of forcing a single combo unit.

03

A separate water closet adds privacy

Where space allows, enclosing the toilet in its own small room keeps the rest of the ensuite feeling like a spa rather than a bathroom.

Before your consultation

A few things worth thinking about first

04

Borrowing space from an adjacent closet is common

If your ensuite footprint is tight, taking a few feet from an adjoining walk-in closet is one of the more common ways to fit a double vanity or separate shower — worth measuring both rooms together.

05

In-floor heating pays off most in an ensuite

Since an ensuite typically gets used barefoot first thing in the morning, it's the room where in-floor heating tends to feel most worth the added cost.

06

A makeup vanity or seated area is a common add-on

Where floor space allows, a small seated vanity area separate from the main double vanity is a popular addition in ensuite renovations.

Common questions

Ensuite renovation FAQs

Can you fit both a soaker tub and a separate shower in my ensuite?

It depends on your existing footprint — we assess this during the free consultation and can suggest layout changes if the space is tight.

Do you install double vanities?

Yes, double vanities are one of the most common upgrades we install in ensuite renovations, and we handle the additional plumbing rough-in required.

How long does an ensuite renovation take?

Most ensuite renovations take 3–5 weeks depending on scope, since there are typically more fixtures and more plumbing than a secondary bathroom.

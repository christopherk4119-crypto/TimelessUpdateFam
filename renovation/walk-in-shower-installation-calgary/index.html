Design decisions

What actually shapes a walk-in shower

A walk-in shower is one of the most customizable parts of a bathroom — these are the decisions worth making early.

01

Glass style changes the room's whole feel

Frameless glass reads as more open and modern; a fixed panel with a wider gap costs less. We can show you both during your consultation.

02

Linear drains allow a slimmer slope

A linear drain along one edge lets the floor slope in a single direction, which works well with large-format tile that would otherwise need to be cut for a center drain.

03

Niches and benches are easiest to add before tile goes up

A recessed niche or built-in bench needs to be framed and waterproofed as part of the shower structure — deciding on these upfront avoids a compromise later.

Before your consultation

A few things worth thinking about first

04

Grout maintenance is a real long-term cost

Large-format tile with fewer grout lines costs a bit more upfront but is noticeably easier to keep clean than a mosaic pattern with dense grout lines.

05

Water pressure and fixture count should be decided together

A rain shower head, a handheld wand, and body jets all draw water at once if you want them simultaneously — worth confirming your home's supply can handle it before you commit to a fixture package.

06

A bench doesn't have to be built-in

A teak shower bench or a fold-down seat are lower-cost alternatives to a built-in tiled bench if budget is a bigger factor than the fully custom look.

Common questions

Walk-in shower FAQs

Can I get a walk-in shower installed without renovating the whole bathroom?

Yes — a walk-in shower can be installed as a standalone project, replacing an existing shower or tub without touching the vanity or toilet.

What's the difference between frameless and semi-frameless glass?

Frameless glass uses thicker, unframed panels for a cleaner look and costs more; semi-frameless uses thinner glass with a metal frame around the edges and costs less. Both are fully waterproofed the same way.

Do you install curbless walk-in showers?

Yes, where the existing floor structure allows for it — we assess this during your free consultation.

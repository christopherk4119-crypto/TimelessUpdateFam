Design without compromise

Accessibility features that don't look like accessibility features

Most accessible design decisions are structural, not cosmetic — which means the finished bathroom can still look exactly the way you want.

01

Grab bar blocking should go in even if you don't need bars yet

Adding solid wood blocking behind the wall during renovation means grab bars can be installed later without opening the wall again.

02

A curbless shower works for wheelchairs and walkers alike

A zero-threshold entry removes the trip hazard a curb creates, and it works just as well for someone without mobility concerns.

03

Comfort-height toilets and lever handles help everyone

A taller toilet and lever-style faucet handles are easier for anyone with limited grip strength or mobility — small changes with a real day-to-day impact.

Before your consultation

A few things worth thinking about first

04

Plan for the future, not just today

Adding grab bar blocking and a wider doorway now — even if you don't need them yet — avoids opening the walls again later if mobility needs change down the road.

05

Non-slip doesn't have to mean institutional-looking

Textured porcelain tile now comes in the same styles as standard tile, so a slip-resistant floor doesn't need to look different from any other bathroom floor.

06

A shower seat is often paired with a handheld showerhead

A built-in or fold-down shower seat combined with a handheld showerhead on a sliding bar covers most aging-in-place needs without looking like medical equipment.

Common questions

Accessible bathroom FAQs

Can you install grab bar blocking without installing the bars themselves yet?

Yes — we often install the wood blocking behind the wall during renovation so grab bars can be added later without reopening the wall.

Do curbless showers require a different floor structure?

Sometimes — a curbless entry needs the subfloor built down slightly, or the surrounding floor built up, so water drains correctly. We assess this during your free consultation.

Can an accessible bathroom still look like a standard, non-institutional bathroom?

Yes — grab bars, comfort-height fixtures, and curbless showers are all available in standard finishes and don't require an institutional look.

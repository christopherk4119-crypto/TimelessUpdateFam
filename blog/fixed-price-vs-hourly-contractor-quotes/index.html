Fixed-Price vs. Hourly Contractor Quotes: What Calgary Homeowners Should Know

When you're comparing renovation quotes, the total number isn't the only thing that matters — how that number is structured matters just as much. Here's the difference between fixed-price and hourly (or "cost-plus") contractor pricing, and why it affects your project's outcome.

How fixed-price quotes work

With a fixed-price quote, the contractor defines the full scope of work up front — materials, labour, and timeline — and gives you one price for that scope. If the job takes longer than expected for reasons within the contractor's control, that's their risk to absorb, not yours.

How hourly or cost-plus quotes work

Hourly and cost-plus pricing bills you for actual labour hours and materials as the project progresses, often with an estimated range rather than a fixed number. This can work well for projects with a lot of unknowns, but it shifts the financial risk of delays and inefficiencies onto the homeowner.

Why scope creep is the real risk

The biggest source of renovation cost overruns isn't dishonesty — it's poorly defined scope. When "what's included" isn't nailed down before work starts, extra costs creep in through change orders, and hourly billing makes those additions easy to justify without a hard ceiling.

Questions to ask before you sign any quote

  • Is this price fixed, or an estimate that could change?
  • What specifically is included — materials, finishes, permits, cleanup?
  • What happens if something unexpected is found once work starts (e.g. water damage behind a wall)?
  • Is there a warranty on labour and finishes after completion?
  • Is the contractor licensed and insured for this scope of work in Alberta?
